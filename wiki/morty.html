Morteza "Morty" Ateshi is an Iranian-born former soldier, the elder brother of Omran and paternal uncle to Oms. In the recovered records he is the family's loudest surviving link to the country it left behind — and its least willing to admit that it did.

He is, by every account, very easy to like.

Appearance

Morty stands 6 ft 0 in (183 cm), seasoned but still hard, with a deep tan and short white hair he combs to the left every morning without fail. He is always clean-shaven.

He dresses in a military uniform from the Pahlavi era, decades out of date and kept in perfect order: the tunic, the matching trousers, boots worn soft from years of use. Under the collar, tucked out of sight, he wears a gold chain the records describe as tacky, apparently in his presence, apparently without offence.

Personality

Morty is outwardly very friendly — warm, generous with food, quick to laugh and quicker to pour. His English is limited and his accent heavy, and he makes up the difference with volume and his hands.

He is a devoted monarchist and has never stopped being one. He talks about the old Iran the way some people talk about someone they lost, and about the government that replaced it in terms the records do not reproduce. Nothing about this has changed in forty years, and he has no intention of letting it.

He is a gifted storyteller. The records note, without drawing a conclusion, that his stories are rarely told the same way twice.

The radio

Morty keeps an old shortwave radio on the kitchen table, and keeps it on. It is tuned to a Tehran station that stopped broadcasting in 1979.

Visitors mostly hear static. Several of them, separately, report hearing something else underneath it — a man reading the news in Persian, calm and unhurried, about events none of them could place. Morty, asked about it, smiles and says the station is still on the air, and that you only have to listen properly.

Oms is recorded as having heard it once. He did not ask again.

Skills and equipment

Morty carries a Browning Hi-Power, as old as his uniform and maintained the same way. The records describe him as an excellent shot who rarely needs to prove it.

His real gift is with people. He can make a stranger feel like family inside a single meal, and the records credit him with getting answers out of people who had come to the table determined not to give any.
